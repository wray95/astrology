# Three Q-Series Saturn Sequence Cases

## Jeff Bezos

- Birth date: 12 January 1964
- Date-only natal Saturn: approximately 28° Capricorn
- D.E. Shaw vice-president period: 1990
- Amazon founded: 1994
- Exact first Saturn return: approximately 18 February 1993
- Saturn in 1990: Sagittarius
- Saturn at Amazon founding in July 1994: Aquarius

Classification: professional preparation in 1990, exact Saturn return in 1993, company founding after the return in 1994. This supports a possible **return → foundation → delayed realization** sequence. It is not a same-sign-ingress event at the Amazon founding date.

## Nikola Tesla

The supplied dates appear to contain a typo: Tesla moved to the United States and began working for Edison in **1884–1885**, not 1984–1985; Tesla died in 1943.

- Birth date: 10 July 1856
- Date-only natal Saturn: approximately 14° Gemini
- United States/Edison period: 1884–1885
- Exact first Saturn return: approximately 29 August 1885
- Saturn in 1884: Taurus
- Saturn in early 1885: Taurus

Classification: the Edison transition and professional entry occurred around the exact first Saturn return, but Saturn was still in Taurus rather than Gemini. This is an **exact-degree return without a natal-sign transit** in the date-only model.

## Frank Lloyd Wright

- Birth date: 8 June 1867
- Date-only natal Saturn: approximately 27° Libra
- Winslow House / first major independent commission: 1894
- Exact first Saturn return: approximately 23 October 1896
- Saturn in 1894: Libra, approximately 2°–13° during the year

Classification: the first major commission occurred during Saturn's natal-sign passage but before the exact natal degree return. The exact return followed approximately two years later. This is a **sign-phase → later exact-return** sequence.

## Comparative conclusion

These three Q cases are not identical:

- Bezos: exact return before company foundation
- Tesla: exact return around professional transition
- Wright: natal-sign phase before exact return and later maturation

This variation is useful. It prevents the theory from being defined so narrowly that every case must show the same timing. A proper Q test should compare these sequence types against many verified career dates and estimate the distribution of delays.
